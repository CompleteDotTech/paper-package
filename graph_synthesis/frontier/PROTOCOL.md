## H1: Latent-source-conditioned evidence probabilities

Hypothesis: explicitly modeling shared source-quality variables prevents false admissions from distinct but dependent evidence primitives. Supply independent binary latent source states with probabilities q_s. Each primitive is assigned one source and two conditional probabilities p(atom | source=0/1). Primitives are independent only conditional on the latent source states. Marginalize over all source assignments and evaluate the monotone proof DNF conditionally; compare to the previous evaluator using only marginal primitive probabilities. This models declared dependence, not source extraction, causal discovery, or learned document reliability.

Test 96 seeded fixtures with 2-8 atoms, 1-3 sources, 1-10 nonempty proofs of length at most four. Use independent enumeration of the joint latent-plus-primitive assignment space as oracle. Include a shared-source 0.8 case where two perfect conditional copies should give 0.8, not 0.96. Primary conjunction: zero discrepancies above 1e-12, zero false admissions at threshold 0.95, duplicate/order invariance, and at least one prevented false admission. Support caps: 8 sources and 16 atoms; unsupported inputs return explicit staging, never a point estimate. Retain controls where supposedly independent latent sources actually share an unmodeled common cause; correct arithmetic must still be shown to fail under wrong metadata. Save absolute errors for both AND and OR queries, not just favorable OR cases.

## H2: Cost- and noise-aware group-review allocation

Hypothesis: optimizing group contamination reduction under heterogeneous review effort and reviewer mistakes outperforms equal-edge greedy review under an equal effort budget. Reuse the validated 73-development/263-evaluation multicall candidate corpus and the PR #17 single-view development-only risk estimator. No evaluation gold or compact view reaches selection. Supply review cost c_i=min(8, 1+floor(recorded single-arm input tokens/1000)); these are proxy units, not measured minutes or dollars. At assumed sensitivity s0, per-edge detection is s_i=s0/(1+0.05*(c_i-1)); false removal of correct edges is f.

For selected set A, model group contamination q_g(A)=1-product_i[1-p_i*(1-s_i*I(i in A))]. Optimize the sum of contamination reductions minus expected correctly removed edges (unit penalty). Enumerate each group's review subsets up to 12 candidates, then solve an integer-budget multiple-choice knapsack across groups. Groups over the cap receive no optimized review and must be counted. Keep a no-review option. Comparators: PR #17 single-view group-aware order packed into the same effort budget, and marginal-benefit-per-cost greedy with the same noise model. Record no-review outcomes as well.

Primary: budget 40, s0=0.75, f=0.01. Require at least 5% lower observed-label expected contaminated groups than BOTH comparators, with at most 0.25 additional expected correct-edge removals versus either. A failure remains a negative result even when modeled utility improves. Secondary budgets 20/80/160; sensitivity s0 in {0.5,0.75,1} crossed with f in {0,0.01,0.05}, refitting no parameters. All selections are recalculated from features and supplied scenarios only. Report 4,000 paired source-group bootstrap draws, seed above, descriptive 95% and 99% intervals at fixed primary selectors. These omit fitting and reviewer-model uncertainty and are not confirmatory or human-study intervals. Verify optimization against exhaustive subset enumeration on 64 small supplied-risk fixtures; retain a deliberately incorrect-risk control. Do not call proxy-effort savings measured service or human savings.

## H3: Compile-once, state-bounded lineage decision diagrams

Hypothesis: compilation size, rather than a blanket 16-atom limit, permits exact large structured lineage and reusable probability evaluation. Canonicalize monotone DNF, choose a deterministic frequency-descending/lexical atom order, memoize residual formulas and merge identical decision nodes. Compile a reduced ordered binary decision diagram once, then evaluate changing supplied independent primitive probabilities bottom-up. Caps: 512 atoms, 4,096 input proofs, 32,768 visited residual states. Exhausted compilation returns conservative previous lineage bounds with an explicit nonexact status; over input caps stage. Never return a partial exact probability.

Test 128 seeded small formulas against independent exhaustive enumeration, with five probability assignments each. Test shared-fan and adjacent-pair-chain formulas at 17/32/64/128/256 atoms; independent analytic recurrences are their large-case oracles. Evaluate 64 probability updates on one compiled 128-atom chain without recompilation. Primary: zero probability discrepancies above 1e-12; all ten large cases exact, whereas the previous capped evaluator remains nonexact on the connected >16-atom formulas; zero duplicate/order-invariance failures. Include an intentionally tiny state budget, empty proof-list semantics, zero/one probabilities, missing/invalid probabilities and input-cap controls. Report node/state counts and compile/evaluation work separately, not service latency or a worst-case polynomial guarantee. H1 models dependence; H3 alone still assumes independent supplied primitive probabilities.

## H4: Certified bipartite conflict optimization

Hypothesis: recognizing bipartite conflict components and reducing weighted vertex cover to s-t min-cut extends exact optimization beyond the four-cut-vertex limit. For nonnegative integer priorities, create source-to-left and right-to-sink capacities equal to priorities; conflict arcs have capacity sum(priorities)+1. Return the complement of the resulting minimum vertex cover. Emit a capacity-feasible, flow-conserving maximum-flow/minimum-cut certificate and verify it independently. The optimum is supplied-priority utility, not semantic truth.

Support bipartite components through 512 vertices; nonbipartite components use the unchanged PR #17 solver and its staging caps. Compare to that actual prior solver and priority-greedy, not a renamed reimplementation. Test 128 random bipartite graphs up to 12 vertices against independent subset enumeration; 64 general graphs for no prior-utility regression; five complete-bipartite fixtures of 18/32/64/128/256 vertices and four unit-weight grids (4x8,8x8,8x16,16x16) against analytic optima. Primary: zero optimum/certificate/consistency errors, no small-case utility regression, and all nine large supported cases solved exactly. Include tampered-certificate rejection, odd cycles, a dense odd clique, zero weights, duplicate edges, invalid endpoints and the existing false-high-priority semantic negative control.

## H5: Connected-path incremental optimization

Hypothesis: a max-plus segment tree maintains the optimal independent-set utility of a fixed connected path after local priority updates without resolving the whole component. Each vertex contributes the two-state skip/take transfer matrix; internal nodes compose matrices. Validate topology once at construction, enforce nonnegative integer single-vertex updates atomically, and query the root optimum. Full selected-set materialization is separately decoded and remains linear; topology changes require rebuilding, not a misleading constant-time update.

Test 64 random paths (1-64 vertices) with 16 updates each, and a 1,024-vertex path with 256 seeded updates. Compare every optimum and decoded independent-set witness against full linear dynamic programming. Primary: zero utility/witness discrepancies and at least 90% fewer scalar max-plus transition candidates on the large workload, including cold construction. Count eight candidates per internal 2x2 matrix multiplication versus three per vertex in full path DP; also report witness decoding visits and validation work separately. Include repeated/same-value updates, zero weights, invalid update rollback, unknown vertices, nonpath/star/cycle rejection and rebuilt topology. Do not equate this summary-maintenance saving with full graph emission, total CPU, database I/O or service latency.
